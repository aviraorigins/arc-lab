Lesson 1

Claude in 10 minutes

By the end of this lesson you'll know which part of Claude to use for what, which plan you need, and how to write a prompt that gets a usable answer the first time. You'll leave with a 5-line pitch for your own series.

1. Three ways to use Claude

WhereGood forExample from making a series
Chat (claude.ai or the app)Thinking out loud: ideas, scripts, captions, replies"Give me 10 hooks for an episode about a kid who finds a map"
Projects (inside the app)Long jobs where Claude should remember your files and rulesA project holding your story rules, characters and past scripts, so every new script stays consistent
Claude Code (the desktop app's Code tab, or the terminal)Work on your own computer: folders, code and, with the right tools, building videosTelling Claude to render an episode, check every frame and save the video

You can do lessons 1 to 4 in plain Chat. Claude Code comes in lesson 2, because that's where the real production happens.

2. Which plan you need

  • Free: fine for trying Chat, with tighter daily limits.
  • A paid plan, like Pro or Max: needed for Claude Code, with much more room. Max helps when you make episodes every day.
  • An API key from the Claude Console: another way to run Claude Code; you pay for what you use. Set a spend limit in the Console first.

Plans and limits change, so check Claude's own pricing page before you pay.

3. A prompt that works the first time

Most bad answers come from short prompts. Give Claude what you'd give a new teammate: who it's for, what you want, the rules, an example, and the format of the answer. Then react, don't restart: "Make the hook scarier", "Option 2 is weak, give me three new ones".

4. Three habits that save hours

  • One chat per job. A new chat for a new episode keeps old ideas from leaking in. Use a Project for rules that never change.
  • Ask for options, then choose. "Give me 5 versions" beats "give me the best version": you're the director.
  • Ask Claude to check its own work. "Read your script as a stranger who skipped the last episode. What's confusing?"

Do this now (15 minutes)

Open Claude Chat and paste Prompt 1 with your own idea in the brackets. Pick the pitch you like most and paste Prompt 2 to sharpen it. You're done when you have a 5-line pitch you'd actually watch.

Prompt 1: five pitches from one rough idea

I want to start a short video series for Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts. Each episode is 30 to 60 seconds and ends with a vote where viewers choose what happens next.

My rough idea: [describe it in one or two sentences]
My viewers: [who they are, e.g. "18 to 30, like mystery and survival stories"]
Rules: original characters only, simple words, no gore.

Give me 5 different series pitches. For each one: a title, the main character in one line, what they want, what's in their way, and the question viewers will keep coming back to answer.

Prompt 2: sharpen the one you like

I like pitch number [N]. Make it sharper:
- a hook for episode 1 that works in the first 2 seconds, with no setup
- the first vote viewers will see, with 2 options that are both tempting
- one reason someone who missed episode 1 would still watch episode 2
Then write the final pitch in exactly 5 lines.

Prompt 3: test it like a stranger

Pretend you're scrolling Reels and you've never heard of this series. Read the pitch below and tell me honestly:
would you stop scrolling, and why or why not? Then suggest one change that would make you stop.

[paste your 5-line pitch]

Questions

Do I need the Series Skill?

No. The lessons teach you to make content with Claude itself. The Series Skill does the building for you, but every lesson also shows a way to do it without the skill.

Do I need a paid Claude plan?

Not for the first lessons: ideas, pitches and scripts work in free Claude Chat. Making episodes with Claude Code needs a paid Claude plan or an API key.

Will the lessons use Pokémon?

No. Your Trainer Arc is our fan series, but the lessons use Day 23 and our own original characters as examples.
